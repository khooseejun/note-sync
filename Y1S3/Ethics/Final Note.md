## Chapter 1: Introduction to Ethics

### Definition of Ethics
- **Ethics**: A set of values that define acceptable behaviour in a society.
- **Computer Ethics**: Guidelines for the morally acceptable use of computers.
- **Virtue**: A habit that encourages people to do what is right (e.g., fairness, honesty).
- **Vice/Sin**: A habit that encourages wrong behaviour (e.g., envy, rage).

### Ethical Consideration in Decision Making
Most ethical choices involve:
- Several options
- Long-term consequences
- Unpredictable outcomes
- Combined fiscal, legal, and social costs/benefits
- Personal ramifications

**Steps in Ethical Decision Making:**
1.  **Develop Problem Statement**: Succinct explanation of the problem.
2.  **Identify Alternatives**: Engage stakeholders to find multiple solutions.
3.  **Evaluate & Choose Alternative**: Test metrics (efficacy, risk, cost, time).
4.  **Implement Decision**: Execute in a timely, reliable manner.
5.  **Evaluate Results**: Track outcomes and observe effects.

### Difference between Moral, Ethics and Laws
- **Morals**: One's religious views on right/wrong.
- **Ethics**: Rules/codes of conduct a society expects (e.g., professional codes).
- **Law**: Set of laws determining permitted actions (e.g., abortion may be legal but considered immoral by some).

### Common Ethical Issues for IT Users
1.  **Software Piracy**: Making unauthorized copies of software.
2.  **Inappropriate use of computing resources**: Visiting unrelated websites, gaming, sending hate emails.
3.  **Inappropriate sharing of information**: Sharing private (salary, health) or confidential (trade secrets, strategies) data.

### Ethics for IT Professionals
**Characteristics of a good tech career:**
- Patience
- Effective communication
- Ability to adapt quickly
- Multitasking
- Problem-solving ability
- Excited about the job

**Professional Code of Ethics:**
- Outlines ideals and standards for a profession.
- **Advantages**:
    - Ethical decision-making
    - High ethical principles
    - Public interest and confidence
    - Self-evaluation benchmark

## Chapter 2: Computer and Internet Crime

### Types of Cybercrime
Cybercrime is illegal activity over the Internet. It can be classified into:
1.  **Malicious harm** (intentional).
2.  **Accidental harm** (e.g., a "harmless" virus causing disruption).

### Security Attacks

#### 1. Passive Attacks
- **Goal**: Obtain information without harming system resources.
- **Focus**: Prevention (difficult to detect).
- **Examples**:
    - **Eavesdropping**: Secretly listening to conversations.
    - **Traffic monitoring**: Monitoring location, frequency, and duration of messages.

#### 2. Active Attacks
- **Goal**: Tamper with data streams or create false streams.
- **Four Groups**:
    1.  **Masquerade**: One person pretends to be another.
    2.  **Replay**: Passive recording and retransmission of data.
    3.  **Message alteration**: Delaying, reordering, or changing messages.
    4.  **Denial-of-Service (DoS)**: Flooding a system with fake requests.

**Common Active Attack Tools:**
- **Virus**: Code that causes unexpected behaviour; requires user action (e.g., opening an email).
- **Worm**: Self-duplicates without human help.
- **Trojan Horse**: Disguised as legitimate software; does not replicate.
- **Botnet**: Collection of infected computers (zombies) controlled by hackers.
- **DDoS**: Coordinated attack using multiple compromised machines.
- **Rootkit**: Tools allowing remote access and manipulation.
- **Spam**: Unsolicited messages (email, social media, SMS, SEO spam).
- **Phishing**: Social engineering to obtain sensitive information.

## Chapter 6: Risk Assessment and Trustworthy Computing

### CIA Triad (Core Principles)
| Principle | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Confidentiality** | Ensuring data is accessible only to authorized users. |
| **Integrity** | Safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of data. |
| **Availability** | Ensuring designated users have constant access to assets. |

### Trustworthy Computing
A computing approach providing stable, confidential, and consistent interactions based on sound business practices. **Core principles** include:
- Security
- Privacy
- Reliability
- Business integrity

### International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
- **ISO 27001**: A standard providing requirements for an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**.
- **ISMS**: A systematic approach to managing sensitive assets (people, processes, IT systems).
- **14 Categories** include: Access Control, Cryptography, Asset Management, Communications Security.

### National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0**: Guidelines for companies to identify, detect, respond, prevent, and recover from cyberattacks.
- **Three basic parts:**
    1.  **CSF Core**: Defines activities needed for cybersecurity outcomes (Functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover).
    2.  **CSF Organizational Profiles**: Current status vs. target goals ("road maps").
    3.  **CSF Tiers**: Four levels indicating compliance (higher tier = more compliant).

## Chapter 7: Software Development

### Software Development Methodology
A tried-and-true work procedure to produce high-quality software.

**Key Concept: Cost of Defects**
- Identifying a defect early (requirements phase) costs **up to 100 times less** than fixing it after shipping.
- Late discovery requires rework on earlier deliverables and increases communication costs.

**Example: Agile Methodology**
- Flexible and iterative.
- Emphasizes continuous delivery and customer feedback.
- Breaks projects into small, manageable portions.

### Software Testing
Evaluating functionality to identify bugs and ensure requirements are met.

**Types of Testing Methods:**
1.  **Manual Testing**: Testers manually evaluate the software from an end-user perspective.
2.  **Automation Testing**: Using automated tools to run scripts and produce results.
3.  **Dynamic Testing**: Testing code by entering data and comparing results.
    - **Black-box testing**: Tester knows nothing about internal code composition (input/output only).
    - **White-box testing**: Tester understands internal logic and tests all feasible paths.

### Quality Assurance (QA) and Standards
- **Definition**: Approaches used during production to ensure consistent service ("fit for use or purpose").
- **The Deming Cycle (PDCA)**:
    - **Plan**: Set objectives and processes.
    - **Do**: Implement the plan.
    - **Check**: Monitor and check procedures against objectives.
    - **Act**: Take action to improve processes.

### Software Quality Techniques
A high-quality product fulfills **Software Requirements Specifications (SRS)**.
- **Scalable**: Operates on various systems.
- **Usability**: Easy for all consumers to invoke features.
- **Reusability**: Components can be replicated for new apps.
- **Correctness**: SRS specifications are applied properly.
- **Maintainability**: Glitches are easily fixed.

## Chapter 8: Intellectual Property

### Intellectual Property (IP)
Creations of the mind: inventions, literary works, symbols, images, designs used in commerce. IP is an **intangible asset** often more valuable than physical assets.

### Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)
Rights derived from human intellectual activities allowing the owner to benefit from their product and prevent others from using it without permission.

### Types of Intellectual Property

| Type | Description | Governing Law (Malaysia Example) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Patents** | A license granting exclusionary rights to commercialize an invention. | Patents Act 1983 |
| **Trademarks** | A sign distinguishing goods/services of one enterprise from another. Strength spectrum: Generic → Descriptive → Suggestive → Arbitrary → Fanciful/Coined. | Trademarks Act 2019 |
| **Copyrights** | Legal right to exploit original works (literary, artistic, musical) and prevent reproduction. | Copyright Act 1987 (Amended 2022) |
| **Trade Secrets** | Confidential information providing a competitive advantage (e.g., source code, marketing strategy). Protected by contract law, common law, and statutes (e.g., Competition Act 2010, Penal Code). | *No specific statute*; protected indirectly. |

**Key Copyright Duration:**
- **Malaysia**: Life of creator + 50 years.
- **US/EU**: Life of author + 70 years.

### Issues Related to Intellectual Property
1.  **Plagiarism**: Stealing thoughts/vocabulary and claiming them as your own.
2.  **Reverse Engineering**: Dismantling a product to ascertain its structure. Not directly illegal in Malaysia but restricted by other laws.
3.  **Open Source Code**: Publicly available code (modifiable/redistributable) vs. Closed source (private).
4.  **Competitive Intelligence (CI)**: Discovering and analyzing patent/market data to understand competitors and identify opportunities.
5.  **Trademark Infringement**: Illegal use of a trademark leading to deception about a product's source.
6.  **Cybersquatting**: Registering domains identical to a third-party brand to profit from goodwill.
    - *Types*: Identity theft, Name jacking, Typosquatting (misspelled URLs, e.g., "Googgle.com"), Reverse-cybersquatting.
